Unit price = package price ÷ units in the package. Capacity is the maker's stated absorbency in millilitres, taken from product pages (not lab-verified) and shared across every store that sells the same product; blank means no store publishes one. Delivered $/L = delivered unit price ÷ capacity in litres, so lower means more absorbency per dollar, shipping included. "Lowest" marks the cheapest in-stock delivered unit price (shipping to the chosen province included) for that product and size across stores; "Lowest price per product & size" uses the same delivered price.

Delivered = (package price + the store's cheapest standard shipping for an order of just that package to the chosen province) ÷ units in the package, before tax. Bags and samples usually fall under free-shipping minimums, so their delivered price can be far above the shelf price; a bigger mixed order would spread that shipping. "+ shipping" means the store sets shipping at checkout, so that figure is the price per unit before shipping.
